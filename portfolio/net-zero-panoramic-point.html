Net Zero at Panoramic Point

Full structural design for a high-elevation net-zero mountain home with custom framing, long overhangs, and coordinated foundation systems.

Project photography pending.

This project required full structural design for a high-elevation mountain home shaped by net-zero goals, custom architectural detailing, and significant snow loading. The structural approach balanced the owners' preference for dimension lumber where feasible with the performance demands of longer spans, open interior volumes, and high-load roof conditions.

Early framing studies explored opportunities to shorten spans and use conventional dimension lumber economically where it made sense. Engineered lumber, including LVL and PSL members, and structural steel were incorporated where the architecture and loading required greater capacity or stiffness.

The design included a walkout-style lower level, a site retaining wall requiring coordination with the building foundation system, a double-cantilevered exterior deck, long roof overhangs, and a clerestory condition. Custom details were developed to maintain clear load paths while supporting the architectural expression.

Project details are presented in accordance with assumed client confidentiality requirements.
